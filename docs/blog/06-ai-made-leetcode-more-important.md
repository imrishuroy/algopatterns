# Contrarian Take: AI Made LeetCode More Important, Not Less

*Everyone's celebrating the death of LeetCode. They're missing the point entirely.*

---

I keep seeing the same take on Twitter:

"AI solved LeetCode. RIP grinding. Time to focus on soft skills."

It sounds logical. It's also completely backwards.

Here's the argument nobody's making: **AI raised the floor, which raised the ceiling.**

Let me explain.

---

## The Old Game

Before AI, the LeetCode game was simple:

1. Learn the patterns
2. Memorize enough solutions  
3. Get lucky that your interview overlaps with what you memorized

It was a brute-force approach. More hours = better odds.

The best candidates weren't necessarily the best engineers. They were the ones with the most time to grind.

---

## What AI Changed

AI made the "memorize solutions" strategy worthless overnight.

Think about it:

- Can't remember how to implement Dijkstra? AI does.
- Forgot the topological sort template? AI has it.
- Need to recall the binary search boundary conditions? AI knows them all.

The *mechanical* part of coding—the part that memorization helped with—is now free.

Everyone has access to perfect implementation.

**So what's left to compete on?**

---

## The New Competitive Landscape

When everyone has a calculator, arithmetic speed stops mattering. What matters is knowing which numbers to plug in.

The AI equivalent:

When everyone has AI coding assistance, what matters is:

1. **Knowing the right approach** before you ask AI to implement it
2. **Recognizing when AI's solution is wrong** for your context
3. **Debugging and adapting** when things don't work
4. **Communicating reasoning** to humans who evaluate you

Here's the kicker: **These are exactly the skills that LeetCode (done correctly) trains.**

---

## The LeetCode Skills That Matter More Now

### 1. Pattern Recognition Speed

"This is a sliding window problem."

In an interview, you have 45 minutes. AI can implement any algorithm, but *you* have to identify which algorithm applies.

If you spend 20 minutes trying approaches before recognizing the pattern, you fail—even with AI assistance.

LeetCode trains pattern recognition. The more problems you see, the faster you recognize problem shapes. This skill matters *more* now because the implementation bottleneck is gone.

### 2. Problem Decomposition

"Let me break this into smaller pieces..."

AI is great at solving well-defined problems. It's terrible at taking a vague problem and creating that well-defined specification.

When an interviewer gives you a problem, there's often ambiguity:
- What counts as valid input?
- What should happen in edge cases?
- What are we optimizing for?

The decomposition skill—breaking messy problems into clean subproblems—is human work. And it's exactly what you practice when you approach LeetCode problems systematically instead of pattern-matching to memorized solutions.

### 3. Critical Evaluation

"Wait, this solution has a bug when..."

AI produces plausible code. It doesn't guarantee correct code.

The ability to look at a solution—yours, AI's, or a colleague's—and spot issues is more valuable now than ever. Because more code is being generated, and someone has to verify it.

LeetCode develops this. When you trace through solutions, analyze edge cases, and understand *why* code fails, you build the evaluation muscle.

### 4. Debugging Under Uncertainty

"It's failing on this test case. Let me trace through..."

When AI-generated code doesn't work, what do you do? 

If you just ask AI to fix it, it might suggest something that doesn't work either. You could end up in a loop of "try this, try that" without understanding.

The ability to systematically debug—to form hypotheses, test them, and narrow down root causes—is distinctly human and distinctly valuable.

LeetCode builds this. Every time you debug your own solution instead of looking up the answer, you're building the skill.

---

## The Counter-Intuitive Implication

AI handles the easy work. This means **the hard work becomes the differentiator**.

What's hard?

- Recognizing non-obvious patterns
- Adapting solutions to novel constraints
- Evaluating trade-offs in context
- Communicating reasoning clearly
- Debugging efficiently under pressure

These are all things that LeetCode trains—if you do it right.

The people who grinded LeetCode through memorization? They're in trouble.

The people who used LeetCode to build genuine problem-solving intuition? They're more valuable than ever.

---

## How I Changed My Approach

I used to think of LeetCode as "how many problems can I solve."

Now I think of it as "how deeply do I understand the patterns."

**Before:** Solve problem → Check solution → Move on

**Now:** 
1. Struggle with problem (no AI)
2. Solve it or give up after 30 min
3. Study the optimal solution deeply
4. Ask: "What signal told me this approach works?"
5. Find 2-3 similar problems
6. Solve them to verify I understood the pattern

The goal isn't to have solved 500 problems. It's to have **deep pattern intuition for 15-20 core patterns**.

---

## The New LeetCode Meta

If you're still preparing for interviews, here's the meta-game now:

### Spend Less Time On:
- Implementation details (AI handles this)
- Memorizing specific solutions (worthless now)
- Obscure algorithms (Aho-Corasick? Skip it.)
- Syntax perfection (AI autocompletes anyway)

### Spend More Time On:
- Pattern recognition drills (rapid-fire categorization)
- Explaining approaches out loud (communication practice)
- Trade-off analysis (why X over Y)
- Debugging practice (intentionally break solutions, then fix them)
- Novel problem variations (train adaptability)

---

## The Interview of 2026

Here's what I'm seeing in interviews this year:

**More follow-ups.** You solve the problem, then: "What if the input is streaming?" "What if memory is limited?" "How would you parallelize this?"

**More evaluation of reasoning.** "Walk me through why you chose this approach." "What alternatives did you consider?" "What would break this?"

**Less tolerance for hand-wavy answers.** You can't just say "I'd use a hash map." You need to explain why, what the trade-offs are, and when you'd choose differently.

**Higher problem difficulty.** The baseline has shifted. What was "medium" is now "easy." What was "hard" is now "medium."

All of these changes favor deep understanding over shallow memorization. All of these changes reward the skills that serious LeetCode practice builds.

---

## The Real Lesson

AI didn't kill LeetCode. AI killed *one way* of doing LeetCode.

The grind-and-memorize approach is dead. Good riddance.

The deep-pattern-understanding approach is more valuable than ever.

The question isn't whether to do LeetCode. The question is whether you're doing it in a way that builds skills AI can't replicate.

If you're just solving problems, you're doing it wrong.

If you're building intuition, you're exactly where you need to be.

---

## My Prediction

In 2-3 years, the best engineers will be the ones who:

1. Instantly recognize problem patterns
2. Know the trade-offs of different approaches
3. Can evaluate and debug AI-generated code
4. Communicate technical decisions clearly
5. Navigate ambiguity and constraints gracefully

Every single one of these skills can be developed through deliberate LeetCode practice.

The people who stopped practicing because "AI will do it" are going to be behind.

The people who adapted their practice to focus on reasoning over memorization are going to pull ahead.

AI raised the floor. But it raised the ceiling even higher.

Are you keeping up?
